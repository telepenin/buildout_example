# Tesla Model Y Charger Comparison

## Model Y Charging Specs

- **Connector**: NACS (North American Charging Standard)
- **Onboard Charger**: 11.5 kW (48A @ 240V) on all current variants
- **J1772 Compatibility**: Adapter included for legacy Level 2 stations
- **Supercharging**: Up to 250 kW DC fast charging

## Charger Comparison

### Tesla Chargers

#### Tesla Wall Connector (Gen 3)
- **Price**: ~$400
- **Max Power**: 11.5 kW (48A @ 240V)
- **Charging Speed**: ~30–44 miles of range per hour
- **Cable Length**: 24 ft
- **Features**: WiFi, Tesla app integration, power sharing (up to 6 units), NACS connector native
- **Installation**: Hardwired, requires 60A circuit breaker
- **Pros**: Seamless Tesla integration, sleek design, power sharing for multi-car households
- **Cons**: NACS only (no J1772 for non-Tesla EVs), requires hardwiring

#### Tesla Universal Wall Connector
- **Price**: ~$595
- **Max Power**: 11.5 kW (48A @ 240V)
- **Charging Speed**: ~30–44 miles of range per hour
- **Cable Length**: 24 ft
- **Features**: Dual NACS + J1772 connector, WiFi, Tesla app integration, power sharing
- **Installation**: Hardwired, requires 60A circuit breaker
- **Pros**: Charges any EV (Tesla and non-Tesla), future-proof
- **Cons**: Higher price, still requires hardwiring

#### Tesla Mobile Connector
- **Price**: ~$200
- **Max Power**: 7.7 kW (32A @ 240V) with NEMA 14-50 adapter
- **Charging Speed**: ~20–30 miles of range per hour
- **Cable Length**: 20 ft
- **Features**: Portable, swappable NEMA adapters (14-50, 6-50, 14-30, etc.)
- **Installation**: Plug-in, no electrician needed if outlet exists
- **Pros**: Portable, affordable, great for travel or renters, no installation cost
- **Cons**: Slower than 48A options, adapters sold separately (~$35–45 each)

### Third-Party Chargers

#### ChargePoint Home Flex
- **Price**: ~$350–400
- **Max Power**: 12 kW (50A @ 240V)
- **Charging Speed**: ~37 miles of range per hour
- **Cable Length**: 23 ft
- **Features**: WiFi, ChargePoint app, energy usage tracking, adjustable amperage (16–50A), NEMA 14-50 plug or hardwired
- **Installation**: Plug-in (NEMA 14-50) or hardwired
- **Pros**: Flexible install options, adjustable power, strong app, works with all EVs via J1772 (NACS adapter needed for Tesla)
- **Cons**: Requires J1772-to-NACS adapter for Tesla, slightly lower max speed than 48A units

#### Emporia EV Charger
- **Price**: ~$160–200
- **Max Power**: 11.5 kW (48A @ 240V)
- **Charging Speed**: ~30–44 miles of range per hour
- **Cable Length**: 24 ft
- **Features**: WiFi, Emporia app, energy monitoring, charge scheduling, NEMA 14-50 plug
- **Installation**: Plug-in (NEMA 14-50)
- **Pros**: Best value, full 48A charging at a fraction of the cost, easy plug-in install
- **Cons**: Build quality not as premium, newer brand, J1772 connector (adapter needed for Tesla)

#### Grizzl-E Classic
- **Price**: ~$400
- **Max Power**: 9.6 kW (40A @ 240V)
- **Charging Speed**: ~25–30 miles of range per hour
- **Cable Length**: 24 ft
- **Features**: NEMA 4 rated (weatherproof/dustproof), adjustable amperage (16/24/32/40A), UL listed
- **Installation**: NEMA 14-50 plug or hardwired
- **Pros**: Extremely rugged and durable, excellent for outdoor/garage install, no subscription fees
- **Cons**: No WiFi or smart features (smart version ~$500), slower at 40A max, J1772 (adapter needed)

#### Wallbox Pulsar Plus
- **Price**: ~$500–650
- **Max Power**: 11.5 kW (48A @ 240V)
- **Charging Speed**: ~30–44 miles of range per hour
- **Cable Length**: 25 ft
- **Features**: WiFi + Bluetooth, myWallbox app, power sharing/boost, energy scheduling, compact design
- **Installation**: Hardwired
- **Pros**: Very compact form factor, strong app ecosystem, power sharing capability, sleek design
- **Cons**: Higher price, hardwired only, J1772 connector (adapter needed for Tesla)

#### JuiceBox 48
- **Price**: ~$600
- **Max Power**: 11.5 kW (48A @ 240V)
- **Charging Speed**: ~30–44 miles of range per hour
- **Cable Length**: 25 ft
- **Features**: WiFi, JuiceNet app, Alexa/Google Assistant integration, energy scheduling, smart grid ready
- **Installation**: NEMA 14-50 plug or hardwired
- **Pros**: Voice assistant integration, flexible install, smart grid features
- **Cons**: Pricier, Enel X ownership changes have raised support concerns, J1772 (adapter needed)

## Recommendations

- **Best Overall for Tesla**: Tesla Wall Connector — native NACS, seamless app integration, competitive price
- **Best Budget**: Emporia EV Charger — full 48A power for under $200, hard to beat on value
- **Best Portable/Renter-Friendly**: Tesla Mobile Connector — no installation needed, take it anywhere
- **Best for Multi-EV Household**: Tesla Universal Wall Connector — charges any EV with one unit
- **Best Rugged/Outdoor**: Grizzl-E Classic — NEMA 4 rated, built to last in harsh conditions
- **Best Smart Features**: Wallbox Pulsar Plus or ChargePoint Home Flex — strong apps and scheduling

## Notes

- All third-party chargers use J1772 connectors and require a J1772-to-NACS adapter (~$15–30) to charge a Tesla Model Y
- Many chargers now offer NACS versions — check for availability at time of purchase
- A 48A charger requires a 60A circuit breaker (NEC 80% rule)
- Installation costs typically run $200–800 depending on electrical panel distance and local rates
- Check local utility rebates — many offer $200–500 off home EV charger installation
