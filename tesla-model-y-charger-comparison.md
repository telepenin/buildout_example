# Tesla Model Y Charger Comparison

## Model Y Charging Specs

- **Connector**: NACS (North American Charging Standard)
- **Onboard Charger**: 11.5 kW (48A @ 240V) on all current variants
- **J1772 Compatibility**: Adapter included for legacy Level 2 stations
- **Supercharging**: Up to 250 kW DC fast charging (V3/V4 Superchargers)

### Standard Range vs Long Range Charging Differences (2026 Juniper)

| Spec | Standard Range (RWD) | Long Range (AWD) / Performance |
|---|---|---|
| Battery Chemistry | LFP (Lithium Iron Phosphate) | NMC (Nickel Manganese Cobalt) |
| Battery Capacity | ~60–64 kWh nominal | ~78–82 kWh nominal |
| Peak DC Fast Charge Rate | ~225 kW | ~250 kW |
| Supercharger 10–80% Time | ~32–38 minutes | ~25–30 minutes |
| Max AC Charging Rate (home) | 11.5 kW (48A) | 11.5 kW (48A) |
| Daily Charge Target | 100% (LFP safe at full) | 70–90% recommended |

Both trims charge at the same maximum AC rate at home (11.5 kW / 48A), so the home charger experience is identical. Differences show up primarily at DC fast chargers (Superchargers) and in total range.

## Charger Comparison

### Tesla Chargers

#### Tesla Wall Connector (Gen 3)
- **Price**: ~$425–445
- **Max Power**: 11.5 kW (48A @ 240V)
- **Charging Speed**: Up to 44 miles of range per hour; full charge overnight in ~8 hours
- **Cable Length**: 24 ft
- **Connector**: NACS (native)
- **Installation**: Hardwired, requires 60A circuit breaker
- **Features**: WiFi with OTA firmware updates, Tesla app integration, auto-sensing handle (opens charge port automatically), power sharing across up to 6 units, indoor/outdoor rated (NEMA 3R), 4-year warranty
- **Pros**: Seamless Tesla ecosystem integration, auto-open charge port, sleek compact design, most affordable 48A option, power sharing for multi-car households
- **Cons**: NACS only (non-Tesla EVs need adapter), must be hardwired (no plug-in option), no ENERGY STAR certification, no whole-home energy monitoring or TOU rate tracking

#### Tesla Universal Wall Connector
- **Price**: ~$595
- **Max Power**: 11.5 kW (48A @ 240V)
- **Charging Speed**: Up to 44 miles of range per hour
- **Cable Length**: 24 ft
- **Connector**: Dual NACS + J1772
- **Installation**: Hardwired, requires 60A circuit breaker
- **Features**: WiFi, Tesla app integration, power sharing, charges any EV
- **Pros**: Charges any EV (Tesla and non-Tesla) with one unit, future-proof
- **Cons**: Higher price, still requires hardwiring

#### Tesla Mobile Connector (Gen 3)
- **Price**: ~$300 (included with some new 2026 Model Y purchases)
- **Max Power**: 7.7 kW (32A @ 240V) with NEMA 14-50 adapter; 1.4 kW (12A @ 120V) with NEMA 5-15
- **Charging Speed**: ~30 mi/hr (240V) or ~3 mi/hr (120V)
- **Cable Length**: 20 ft
- **Connector**: NACS
- **Installation**: Plug-in, no electrician needed if outlet exists
- **Included Adapters**: NEMA 5-15 and NEMA 14-50 (additional adapters like 6-50, 14-30, TT-30 sold separately)
- **Features**: Portable (fits in storage bag), Gen 3 supports PowerShare (V2L/V2V) on compatible vehicles
- **Pros**: Portable and versatile for travel, included with many new Teslas, good backup solution, PowerShare capability
- **Cons**: Slower than 48A options (32A max), no WiFi or smart features, additional outlet adapters sold separately (~$35–45 each)

#### Tesla Supercharger Network
- **Price**: Pay-per-use; typically $0.25–0.50/kWh depending on location and time
- **Max Power**: V3: up to 250 kW; V4: up to 250 kW for Model Y (up to 500 kW for 800V vehicles)
- **Charging Speed**: 10–80% in approximately 25–38 minutes depending on trim
- **Features**: 15,000+ stations in North America, automatic billing, route planning with battery preconditioning, V4 stations support CCS for non-Tesla vehicles
- **Pros**: Fastest charging option, extremely reliable network, seamless plug-and-charge, integrated trip planner
- **Cons**: Most expensive per-kWh method, frequent DC fast charging can accelerate battery degradation, idle fees if car remains plugged in after completion

### Third-Party Chargers

#### ChargePoint Home Flex
- **Price**: ~$549–699
- **Max Power**: 12 kW (50A @ 240V), adjustable 16–50A
- **Charging Speed**: Up to ~44 mi/hr (Model Y caps at 48A/11.5 kW so 50A capability is unused)
- **Cable Length**: 23 ft
- **Connector**: Available in J1772 or NACS; cable is swappable between the two
- **Installation**: Plug-in (NEMA 14-50, limited to 40A) or hardwired
- **Features**: WiFi, ChargePoint app, energy usage tracking, TOU rate awareness, adjustable amperage, ENERGY STAR certified, 3-year warranty
- **Pros**: Most polished app experience, industry-leading 50A output, swappable connector cable (future-proof), well-established brand, UL listed and ENERGY STAR certified
- **Cons**: Most expensive option, 23 ft cable is shortest on this list, 50A is overkill for Model Y, requires professional install for hardwired 50A

#### Emporia EV Charger Classic
- **Price**: ~$429
- **Max Power**: 11.5 kW (48A @ 240V) hardwired; 9.6 kW (40A) via NEMA 14-50
- **Charging Speed**: Up to ~44 miles of range per hour (48A hardwired)
- **Cable Length**: 24 ft
- **Connector**: J1772 or NACS available
- **Installation**: Plug-in (NEMA 14-50) or hardwired
- **Features**: WiFi, Emporia app, scheduling, energy monitoring, ENERGY STAR certified, indoor/outdoor rated
- **Pros**: Strong value at full 48A, NACS version auto-opens Tesla charge port, excellent cold-weather cable performance, ENERGY STAR certified
- **Cons**: WiFi can be inconsistent, app less polished than ChargePoint/Wallbox, connector holster collects water/debris

#### Emporia EV Charger Pro
- **Price**: ~$599
- **Max Power**: 11.5 kW (48A @ 240V) hardwired; 9.6 kW (40A) via NEMA 14-50
- **Charging Speed**: Up to ~44 miles of range per hour (48A hardwired)
- **Cable Length**: 25 ft
- **Connector**: J1772 or NACS available
- **Installation**: Plug-in (NEMA 14-50) or hardwired
- **Features**: Everything in Classic, plus **PowerSmart technology** with Vue 3 energy monitor — automatically adjusts charging based on whole-home electrical load (avoids tripped breakers, no panel upgrade needed), excess solar charging mode
- **Pros**: PowerSmart load management is a standout feature (can save thousands on panel upgrades), solar surplus charging, named "best overall" EV charger by Car Talk 2026
- **Cons**: Same app/holster issues as Classic, higher price

#### Grizzl-E Classic / Classic Connect
- **Price**: ~$300 (Classic non-smart) / ~$350 (Classic Connect with WiFi)
- **Max Power**: 9.6 kW (40A @ 240V), adjustable 16–40A
- **Charging Speed**: ~28–32 miles of range per hour
- **Cable Length**: 24–25 ft
- **Connector**: J1772 or NACS (NACS available on Connect model)
- **Installation**: NEMA 14-50 plug or hardwired
- **Features**: NEMA 4 rated cast aluminum enclosure (waterproof, fire-resistant, -30°C to +50°C), WiFi and app (Connect model only), designed and assembled in Canada, 3-year warranty (5-year for +$100)
- **Pros**: Exceptional value, tank-like build quality (cast aluminum), long cable, no-frills reliability
- **Cons**: Capped at 40A (charges ~15% slower than 48A chargers), smart features only on Connect model, less polished app, industrial appearance

#### Wallbox Pulsar Plus
- **Price**: ~$649
- **Max Power**: 11.5 kW (48A @ 240V) hardwired; 9.6 kW (40A) via NEMA 14-50
- **Charging Speed**: Up to ~44 miles of range per hour (48A)
- **Cable Length**: 25 ft (longest on this list)
- **Connector**: Available in J1772 or NACS
- **Installation**: Hardwired or NEMA 14-50 plug
- **Features**: WiFi + Bluetooth, Wallbox app with scheduling/energy monitoring/usage reports, ENERGY STAR certified, NEMA Type 4 rated (-22°F to 122°F), extremely compact (~8×8×4 inches), adjustable 6–48A
- **Pros**: Most compact and attractive design, longest cable, excellent app with intuitive scheduling, cable wraps around unit for neat storage, ENERGY STAR certified
- **Cons**: Cable stiffens in extreme cold, connector holster feels flimsy, higher price, no panel load management

#### JuiceBox 48 (Enel X) — DISCONTINUED
- **Price**: Was ~$600 (limited remaining stock only)
- **Max Power**: 11.5 kW (48A @ 240V)
- **Charging Speed**: Up to ~44 miles of range per hour
- **Cable Length**: 25 ft
- **Connector**: J1772 only (no NACS version produced)
- **Features**: WiFi, JuiceNet app, Alexa/Google Assistant, smart grid scheduling
- **Status**: **Discontinued** — Enel X ceased production. App support and cloud services may be at risk. **Not recommended for new purchases.**
- **Alternatives**: Consider the Emporia Pro or Wallbox Pulsar Plus in this price range.

## Recommendations

- **Best Overall for Tesla**: Tesla Wall Connector ($425–445) — cheapest 48A option, seamless integration, auto-open charge port
- **Best Budget**: Grizzl-E Classic Connect ($350) — rugged build, good cable, but limited to 40A
- **Best Value at 48A**: Emporia Classic ($429) — full 48A, NACS available, ENERGY STAR, auto-opens Tesla charge port
- **Best Smart Features / Future-Proofing**: ChargePoint Home Flex ($549–699) — swappable J1772/NACS cable, best app, 50A output
- **Best for Limited Electrical Panels**: Emporia Pro ($599) — PowerSmart load management can eliminate costly panel upgrades
- **Best Portable / Renter-Friendly**: Tesla Mobile Connector ($300) — no installation needed, take it anywhere
- **Best for Multi-EV Household**: Tesla Universal Wall Connector ($595) — charges any EV with one unit
- **Best Rugged / Outdoor**: Grizzl-E Classic ($300–350) — NEMA 4 cast aluminum, built for harsh conditions
- **Best Design & Cable Length**: Wallbox Pulsar Plus ($649) — most compact, 25 ft cable, attractive design

## Notes

- All third-party chargers now offer NACS connector versions — check availability at time of purchase
- If using a J1772 charger with Tesla, you need a J1772-to-NACS adapter (~$15–30)
- A 48A charger requires a 60A circuit breaker (NEC 80% continuous load rule)
- Installation costs typically run $200–800 depending on electrical panel distance and local rates
- Check local utility rebates — many offer $200–500 off home EV charger installation
- JuiceBox 48 is discontinued and no longer recommended for new purchases
